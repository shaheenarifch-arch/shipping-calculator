Customs duty in Pakistan explained

Every parcel entering Pakistan can be charged customs duty and taxes. This is the part most people worry about, so here is the simple version.

How duty is calculated

Duty is based on the declared value of your items and their product category. Clothing, shoes, cosmetics, perfume and electronics are treated differently. In our live tests, duty and taxes on clothing came to roughly 40% to 50% of the declared value. For example, a $100 order of kids' clothing showed estimated duty of about $45 to $55, and a $300 order of women's clothing showed about $119 to $141, depending on the courier.

Pay now (DDP) or pay on delivery (DDU)?

  • DDU, duty paid on delivery: you pay only shipping now, and the courier collects duty from you when the parcel arrives. Cheaper up front, but the parcel can be held until you pay.
  • DDP, duty paid up front: you pay shipping and duty together, so nothing is due at your door. Fewer surprises and faster customs clearance.

Our calculator shows both. Tick "Include customs duty & taxes now" to see duty-paid prices.

Always declare the real value of your items. Under-declaring can lead to fines, delays or seizure, and it is never worth the risk. Duty rules are set by Pakistan Customs and can change; our estimates come from the courier systems and are a guide, not a guarantee.

The Rs 1,000 duty-free limit

Since 1 July 2025, the duty-free limit for courier parcels entering Pakistan is only Rs 1,000, down from Rs 5,000. That means almost every real purchase from a US store is taxable, and once a parcel is above the limit, duty is charged on the full value. A parcel can attract several layers: customs duty based on the product's tariff code, additional customs duty, regulatory duty on some "non-essential" items such as cosmetics and fashion, and sales tax of around 18% on the duty-paid value. For the full breakdown, read our guide to US import costs to Pakistan in 2026.

Your total landed cost

The price that matters is the landed cost: everything you pay to get the item into your hands in Pakistan. It has five parts:

  1. Product price at the US store, ideally on sale.
  2. US sales tax, which depends on the state. In Massachusetts, where our US address is, clothing items up to $175 are exempt from sales tax, a real saving on apparel.
  3. International shipping, based on actual or volumetric weight, whichever is higher.
  4. Pakistani duty and taxes, as explained above.
  5. Our small service charge for receiving, repacking and forwarding your parcel.

As a rule of thumb, a small order of a single item can end up costing around twice the US shelf price once shipping and duty are added. Bigger, combined orders bring the cost per item down, which is why consolidating purchases is the most effective way to save. Mobile phones are a special case: every imported phone must be registered on PTA's DIRBS system and the PTA tax applies whatever the price, so always check the tax for your model before buying.
